# Writing

Writing is the technology of persistent external memory. It enables information to survive individual agents, travel across space and time, and accumulate into complex traditions. It is not a transparent representation of thought but a medium with its own constraints and affordances.

## Writing as Technology

Writing requires:

- **Physical medium** - Clay, stone, parchment, paper, metal, wood
- **Tools** - Styluses, brushes, pens, chisels
- **Writing system** - A convention mapping language to persistent marks
- **Skills** - Physical motor patterns for producing legible marks
- **Social infrastructure** - Scribes, schools, libraries, copying institutions

Each of these has history, variation, and failure modes.

## Writing and Knowledge

Writing transforms knowledge:

- It enables preservation across generations
- It enables transmission across distances
- It enables accumulation (later scholars can build on earlier work)
- It enables standardization (a single text can be copied many times)
- It enables new error modes (copying mistakes, misreading, forgery)

Some knowledge cannot be fully captured in writing:

- Procedural knowledge (how to ride a horse)
- Perceptual expertise (how to recognize a diseased plant)
- Motor skill (how to forge a blade)

## Writing and Language

Writing systems are not language. They are technologies for encoding language. The relationship between writing and speech is complex:

- Writing may preserve older forms while speech changes
- Writing may be used for languages different from the scribe's native speech
- Writing may create prestige standards that influence spoken norms
- Writing may be phonetic, logographic, or mixed

## Writing and Magic

Mass-distributed symbols may interact with mana. In particular:

- Repeated ritual texts create stable symbolic patterns
- Bureaucratic forms create massive repeated informational structures
- Copying errors may introduce variants that become standardized and magically relevant
