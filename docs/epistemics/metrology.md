# Metrology

Metrology is the science of measurement systems: units, standards, calibration, and the social institutions that maintain them. It is a foundational domain because measurement enables comparison, trade, technology, and scientific progress.

## Socially Constructed Units

Different societies may use different units for the same physical quantity. A length may be measured in body parts, agricultural furrows, standardized rods, or abstract numerical units. The choice of unit system affects:

- Ease of calculation
- Precision of expression
- Compatibility with other communities
- Ritual or symbolic associations

## Calibration Provenance

Measurements are only as good as their calibration. A calibration chain traces back to a reference standard:

```text
local workshop measure
↓
calibrated against guild standard
↓
calibrated against city standard
↓
calibrated against royal standard
↓
maintained by appointed keeper
```

Breaks in this chain introduce systematic error. Different communities with different standards may produce incompatible measurements.

## Standardization and Change

Standardization reforms may be driven by:

- Political unification
- Trade expansion
- Scientific advancement
- Bureaucratic rationalization
- Magical necessity

Changing units may unintentionally alter magical practices if the old units were embedded in ritual or craft traditions that created stable mana patterns.

## Error and Uncertainty

Real measurement systems have error. The architecture must model:

- Random error (unpredictable variation)
- Systematic error (consistent bias)
- Precision limits (smallest discriminable difference)
- Calibration drift (standards changing over time)
