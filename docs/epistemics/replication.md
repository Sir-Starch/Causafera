# Replication

Replication is the independent repetition of an experiment or observation to verify its results. It is the foundation of reliable knowledge, distinguishing genuine findings from error, fraud, or coincidence.

## Why Replication Matters

A single experiment may fail for many reasons:

- Random variation produced an unusual outcome
- The experimenter made a mistake
- The instrument malfunctioned
- The hypothesis was wrong but the data happened to match
- The experimenter selectively reported results

Replication reduces these risks by requiring the same result to appear under different conditions.

## Types of Replication

- **Direct replication** - Repeating the same experiment with the same methods
- **Conceptual replication** - Testing the same hypothesis with different methods
- **Independent replication** - Different researchers, different locations, different instruments
- **Systematic replication** - Varying one parameter at a time to establish boundary conditions

## Replication Culture

Whether replication occurs depends on social institutions:

- Are raw data preserved and shared?
- Are methods documented in sufficient detail?
- Do institutions reward replication or only novelty?
- Is there social pressure to confirm or challenge findings?
- Are there resources available for replication studies?

A society with weak replication culture may accumulate false findings that persist for generations.

## Replication Failure

When replication fails, several responses are possible:

- The original finding is rejected
- The original finding is qualified (works only under specific conditions)
- The replication is rejected (the replicators made a mistake)
- Both are preserved as competing claims

The response depends on the relative credibility of the original researchers and the replicators, the institutions involved, and the social stakes of the finding.

## Document Provenance and Replication

Documents play a crucial role in replication. A well-documented experiment specifies:

- What was done
- With what materials and instruments
- Under what conditions
- Producing what observations
- Analyzed by what methods

Poor documentation makes replication impossible even when the original finding was genuine.
