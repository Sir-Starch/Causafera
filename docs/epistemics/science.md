# Science

Science is institutionalized knowledge-seeking: the social practices, organizations, and norms that enable systematic observation, hypothesis testing, and knowledge accumulation. It is not a technology tree or a linear progression toward truth.

## Science as Social Practice

Science requires:

- **Individuals** who observe, hypothesize, and experiment
- **Communities** who evaluate, criticize, and build on each other's work
- **Institutions** who fund, organize, and preserve knowledge
- **Norms** about what counts as evidence, argument, and proof

Different societies may have different scientific institutions and norms. What counts as convincing evidence in one society may be dismissed in another.

## Scientific Progress

Scientific progress is not guaranteed. It depends on:

- Resources available for investigation
- Social prestige of knowledge-seeking
- Institutional support for replication and criticism
- Communication infrastructure for sharing findings
- Educational systems for training researchers
- Cultural tolerance for uncertainty and revision

Societies may stagnate, regress, or develop in locally optimal but globally limited directions.

## Scientific Error

Science is fallible. Common failure modes:

- **Confirmation bias** - Seeking evidence that supports existing beliefs
- **Authority bias** - Accepting findings because of who produced them
- **Prestige bias** - Favoring research from prestigious institutions
- **Theoretical entrenchment** - Resisting paradigm change
- **Fraud** - Deliberate data fabrication
- **Measurement artifact** - Mistaking instrument behavior for natural phenomenon

These are not bugs to be eliminated. They are essential features of bounded human cognition that shape the actual history of knowledge.

## Science and Magic

In a mana-sensitive world, scientific practices may have magical consequences:

- Laboratory spaces accumulate mana from repeated similar practices
- Standardized measurement creates temporal recurrence
- Scientific institutions produce massive repeated informational structures
- Scientific terminology creates stable symbolic patterns

A scientific revolution may therefore have magical side effects unrelated to its epistemic content.
