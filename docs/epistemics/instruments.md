# Instruments

Instruments are physical tools that extend human perceptual or measurement capabilities. They are not transparent windows onto reality. Each instrument has properties, limitations, and a social context of use.

## Instrument Types

The architecture must support:

- **Length measures** - Rulers, rods, chains, odometers
- **Mass measures** - Balances, weights, scales
- **Time measures** - Sundials, water clocks, mechanical clocks, hourglasses
- **Temperature measures** - Thermoscopes, thermometers, pyrometers
- **Optical instruments** - Lenses, mirrors, microscopes, telescopes
- **Acoustic instruments** - Resonators, tuning devices
- **Mana-sensitive instruments** - Tools that detect or measure mana-related properties

## Instrument Properties

Each instrument has:

- **Physical construction** - Materials, geometry, mechanism
- **Operating principle** - How it transduces the measured property into a perceptible output
- **Range** - The span of values it can measure
- **Resolution** - The smallest difference it can discriminate
- **Accuracy** - How close its readings are to true values
- **Reliability** - Consistency across repeated use
- **Maintenance requirements** - What keeps it functioning correctly
- **Cost and availability** - Who can afford to make or buy it

## Instrument and Knowledge

Instruments do not merely extend perception. They shape what can be perceived:

- A microscope reveals microorganisms, enabling germ theory
- A precise balance reveals mass conservation, enabling quantitative chemistry
- A telescope reveals celestial detail, enabling new astronomy

But instruments also create artifacts and errors:

- Chromatic aberration in early lenses
- Systematic bias in poorly calibrated balances
- Observer effects in mana-sensitive devices

## Instrument Provenance

Instruments have histories:

- Who made them
- What materials were used
- What techniques were employed
- How they have been maintained
- What measurements they have participated in

This provenance matters for assessing reliability and for understanding how measurement practices evolved.
