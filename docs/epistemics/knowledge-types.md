# Knowledge Types

Not all knowledge is the same. The epistemic system must distinguish types of knowledge that differ in how they are acquired, stored, transmitted, and applied.

## Declarative Knowledge

Knowledge that something is the case. Facts, propositions, theories.

Examples:

- Water freezes when cold enough
- Mixing these substances produces heat
- The sun rises in the east

Declarative knowledge is relatively easy to transmit through language and documents. It is also relatively easy to transmit incorrectly.

## Procedural Knowledge

Knowledge of how to do something. Skills, techniques, recipes, methods.

Examples:

- How to forge a blade
- How to prepare a medicinal compound
- How to navigate by stars

Procedural knowledge is harder to transmit than declarative knowledge. It often requires demonstration, practice, and feedback. Written recipes may omit crucial tacit knowledge.

## Perceptual Expertise

Knowledge of how to perceive and interpret complex patterns.

Examples:

- Recognizing diseased tissue by appearance
- Distinguishing ore quality by visual texture
- Detecting weather changes from cloud patterns

Perceptual expertise requires extensive exposure to examples. It cannot be fully transmitted through verbal description.

## Motor Skill

Knowledge embodied in physical action patterns.

Examples:

- Sword fighting techniques
- Musical instrument performance
- Precision craft movements

Motor skill requires physical practice and cannot be acquired from documents alone.

## Knowledge Transfer Limitations

An isekai arrival may possess declarative knowledge from Earth:

> Microorganisms can cause disease.

This does not automatically provide:

- Microscopes
- Glass purity
- Sterile tools
- Experimental infrastructure
- Scientific credibility

The arrival has a foreign prior and a search direction. Actual capability requires the full stack of knowledge types plus material and social prerequisites.

## Knowledge Loss

Societies can lose knowledge. A specialist dies without training a successor. A library burns. A language of technical documentation becomes extinct. The declarative knowledge may survive in fragments while the procedural, perceptual, and motor components are irretrievably lost.
