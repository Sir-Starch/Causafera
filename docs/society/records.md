# Records

Records are persistent documents that preserve information across time and space. They are not simple data storage. They have physical form, provenance, and interpretive history.

## Record Properties

A record may have:

- physical medium (paper, parchment, stone, etc.);
- writing system and script;
- creation date and context;
- author or issuing authority;
- intended audience;
- preservation history;
- damage or loss;
- copies and derivatives;
- provenance chain.

## Record Types

Records may include:

- legal documents (laws, contracts, judgments);
- administrative records (taxes, censuses, permits);
- personal documents (letters, diaries, contracts);
- institutional records (minutes, accounts, inventories);
- religious texts (scriptures, rituals, prophecies);
- scientific documents (observations, experiments, theories);
- maps and geographic descriptions.

## Record Dynamics

Records may be:

- copied (with potential errors);
- damaged (partially or completely);
- misread (interpreted incorrectly);
- normalized (edited to fit conventions);
- translated (mapped to different languages);
- edited (revised or updated);
- partially lost (surviving in fragments);
- forged (created deceptively).

## Copying Errors and Consequences

Copying errors may modify transmitted practices:

```text
three repetitions
↓
copying error
↓
eight repetitions
↓
institutional standardization
↓
new stable pattern
↓
mana response
```
