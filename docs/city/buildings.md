# Buildings

Buildings are physical structures constructed on parcels. They are not static geometry. They have histories, material provenance, and lifecycle states.

## Lifecycle

A building must not merely exist. The architecture must account for:

- construction - materials, labor, techniques, timing;
- material provenance - where components originated;
- use - how spaces are occupied and modified;
- maintenance - upkeep, repairs, deterioration;
- modification - expansions, renovations, repurposing;
- damage - fire, weather, structural failure, conflict;
- abandonment - disuse, vacancy, decay;
- collapse - structural failure and debris;
- reuse - salvage, archaeological residue.

## Material Provenance

Materials maintain geographic provenance through the chain:

```text
geological formation
→ deposit
→ quarry
→ extraction lot
→ transport batch
→ merchant inventory
→ workshop
→ building component
```

This provenance may affect magical properties, structural behavior, and historical analysis.

## Interior Spaces

Buildings contain interior spaces with:

- boundaries and connections;
- lighting and ventilation;
- temperature and humidity;
- occupancy and use patterns;
- contents and inventory;
- fire and safety characteristics.
