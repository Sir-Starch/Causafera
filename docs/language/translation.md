# Translation

Translation is mapping between subjective conceptual systems. It is not perfect semantic replacement. The translator maps source concepts to target conceptual approximations, then encodes those approximations in the target language.

## The Translation Pipeline

```text
source communicative structure
↓
source concepts
↓
translator concept mappings
↓
target conceptual approximations
↓
target language encoding
```

## Concept Gaps

Concept gaps must exist. A foreign concept may map poorly or not at all to the translator's conceptual system.

Example Earth concept:

```text
computer
```

Potential local conceptual approximations:

```text
thinking mechanism
calculation device
memory machine
thinking loom
```

Different translators may produce different mappings based on their own conceptual systems, technical knowledge, and cultural background.

## Translation Effects

Translations may create:

- **Loanwords** - Direct adoption of foreign phonological forms
- **New concepts** - Target language speakers developing concepts they lacked
- **Semantic distortions** - Source concepts mapped to partially incompatible target concepts
- **New technological directions** - Mistranslated concepts inspiring novel approaches

## Isekai Translation

This is especially important for isekai arrivals. An Earth-native arriving in Ontopolis brings conceptual priors from a radically different technological and cultural context. Their attempts to explain Earth concepts may:

- Introduce entirely new conceptual distinctions
- Be misunderstood in locally predictable ways
- Create loanwords that spread through the population
- Inspire local researchers to pursue lines of inquiry they would not otherwise have considered

Example:

```text
Earth concept: "electricity"
Local approximation: "invisible fire that moves through metal"
Result: Local researchers attempt to find "fire-like" properties in conductive materials,
        potentially developing a completely different technological path
```

## Observer Translation

The observer layer's translation of simulation concepts into English is itself a form of translation. It must respect the same limitations: approximate, uncertain, and culturally situated.
