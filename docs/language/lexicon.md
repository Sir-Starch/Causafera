# Lexicon

The lexicon is the set of lexical items (lexemes) that exist within a language community. It is not a dictionary with definitions. It is a population of form lineages that agents associate with concepts through individual learning and social transmission.

## Lexeme as Social Lineage

A lexeme represents a socially transmitted linguistic form lineage. It persists across individual agents through communication, imitation, and documentation. A lexeme has:

- A phonological form (or forms, if sound change has produced variants)
- A history of use within communities
- Morphological properties (can it be inflected? compounded?)
- Social register associations

It does not have a single objective meaning.

## Lexeme Lifecycle

```text
coinage (by an individual agent)
↓
initial use (speaker and immediate audience)
↓
community adoption (spread through social network)
↓
established use (recognized by most community members)
↓
semantic shift (associations drift over time)
↓
archaic / obsolete (falling out of active use)
↓
historical residue (preserved in documents, fossilized expressions)
```

## Lexical Competition

Multiple lexemes may compete to express similar conceptual distinctions. Competition resolves through social dynamics: frequency of use, prestige of speakers, situational appropriateness, and phonological ease.

## Lexical Loss

Lexemes may be forgotten by individual agents or fall out of use across entire communities. Loss creates gaps that may be filled by borrowing, innovation, or semantic expansion of existing forms.

## Lexical Stratification

Communities often maintain multiple lexical layers:

- Core vocabulary (high frequency, stable)
- Specialized terminology (occupational, technical)
- Register markers (formal, informal, ritual)
- Borrowings (from contact languages)
- Archaisms (preserved in specific contexts)

## Observer Glossing

The observer layer may produce human-readable glosses for lexemes. These glosses are analytical approximations, not simulation state. An English gloss such as "water" or "house" is a convenience for the human observer. The simulation stores only internal IDs and generated phonological forms.
